# Panchatantra Storybook

A three-page course in the Panchatantra, built to be lived rather than read once.
**Read it online: [jarvisss007.github.io/panchatantra-storybook](https://jarvisss007.github.io/panchatantra-storybook/)**, or open `index.html` in any browser. No build step; every illustration is inline SVG.

| Page | What it is |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The five books, one illustrated story each, scene by scene, with the teaching of each book and a thirty-day practice tracker. |
| `stories.html` | The library of nested tales, 48 of them, each with its teaching, what it looks like in your life, and the question to ask. Filter by book or theme; pick one for the day. |
| `the-way.html` | The deeper teaching: how the text sees the world, what niti means, the five skills and their shadows, the four means and six measures, your life area by area, a fifteen-question audit, and a ninety-day program. |

## How to open it

- **Online.** [jarvisss007.github.io/panchatantra-storybook](https://jarvisss007.github.io/panchatantra-storybook/), served by GitHub Pages from `main`.
- **Offline.** Download the repo as a zip, unzip it, and open `index.html`. Everything works offline except the two Google Fonts, which fall back to system serif.

## The five books

| Book | Story | Teaching |
|---|---|---|
| Mitra-bheda | The lion, the bull and the jackal | The one who whispers is more dangerous than the one who roars. |
| Mitra-samprapti | The crow, the mouse, the tortoise and the deer | Many weak together are stronger than one strong alone. |
| Kakolukiya | Crows and owls | Know your enemy's nature. A natural foe's peace is a trap. |
| Labdha-pranasha | The monkey and the crocodile | What is in hand is not traded for what is promised. |
| Apariksita-karaka | The brahmin and the mongoose | Look before you act. |

## Daily practice

Five animals, five questions. Keep them where you will see them each morning.

- **Jackal.** Who gains if I believe this?
- **Crow and mouse.** Who has a strength I lack, and have I kept that friendship?
- **Owl.** Is this sudden friend a natural rival?
- **Monkey.** Am I trading what I have for a promise?
- **Mongoose.** Have I seen it, or do I only feel it?

At night, one line: which animal showed up today, and did I act like the hero or the fool?

## Ninety days

1. **See.** One story a day, retold aloud. Log the animal each night.
2. **Act.** One deliberate application per week, per book.
3. **Teach.** Tell the stories to someone. You own only what you can give away.

Progress, audit answers and the daily log stay in the browser you use; nothing is sent anywhere.
