# Panchatantra Storybook

An illustrated, scene-by-scene reading of the five books of the Panchatantra.
Each book opens as a folio with four painted scenes, the teaching of the book
as a maxim, and one question to carry into the day.

Open `index.html` in any browser. There is no build step and no dependency
beyond two Google Fonts; every illustration is inline SVG.

## The five books

| Book | Story | Teaching |
|---|---|---|
| Mitra-bheda | The lion, the bull and the jackal | The one who whispers is more dangerous than the one who roars. |
| Mitra-samprapti | The crow, the mouse, the tortoise and the deer | Many weak together are stronger than one strong alone. |
| Kakolukiya | Crows and owls | Know your enemy's nature. A natural foe's peace is a trap. |
| Labdha-pranasha | The monkey and the crocodile | What is in hand is not traded for what is promised. |
| Apariksita-karaka | The brahmin and the mongoose | Look before you act. |

## Daily practice

Five animals, five questions. Keep them where you will see them each morning.

- **Jackal.** Who gains if I believe this?
- **Crow and mouse.** Who has a strength I lack, and have I kept that friendship?
- **Owl.** Is this sudden friend a natural rival?
- **Monkey.** Am I trading what I have for a promise?
- **Mongoose.** Have I seen it, or do I only feel it?

At night, one line: which animal showed up today, and did I act like the hero or the fool?
